01The short version

Global Executive. Technopreneur. Educator. Author. Cultural Strategist. Builder of ideas, organizations and possibilities.

Dr. Loretta Chen has spent her career working at the edges where technology meets humanity, where culture meets commerce, and where ideas move between Asia and America.

She is the Founder and CEO of Smobler, an emerging technology company building applied AI and digital solutions for real-world problems. She teaches strategy, leadership and entrepreneurship at universities and in executive education, advises organizations navigating growth and transformation, and is a Straits Times bestselling author.

Her career has never followed a single lane. It has moved between technology and theatre, entrepreneurship and education, boardrooms and classrooms, culture and community — united by a fascination with how people, ideas and organizations transform.

Her work can be best described in four verbs:
  • BuildCompanies, technologies, ventures and possibilities.
  • LeadOrganizations, strategy, transformation and people.
  • TeachExecutives, entrepreneurs, students and emerging leaders.
  • CreateBooks, theatre, culture, ideas and stories that deserve a larger stage.
Underpinning all four is a single philosophy:

From margins to frontier.

Loretta is drawn to the places others overlook — women whose stories have not been told, technologies before they become mainstream, students discovering their voices, ASEAN leaders beyond the official biography, immigrant histories at risk of disappearing, and communities too often left outside the innovation economy.

For her, the edge is not the periphery. It is where the future begins.

Originally from Singapore and now based in Hawaiʻi, Loretta has built a life and career across cultures, disciplines and continents. She holds a doctorate in philosophy from the National University of Singapore, doctoral research at UCLA, and has also studied at UC Davis and the University of London all under full scholarship.

When she isn’t building, teaching, writing or creating the next venture, she lives in East Honolulu, five minutes from the ocean, surrounded by a lush garden and eighteen rescue cats who are unimpressed by her résumé.

03Storyteller

Before the boardroom came the stage. The stories keep coming.

Long before Loretta built companies, taught executives or worked in emerging technology, she told stories.

As an award-winning theatre director, she became known for work that was provocative, human and pushes margins as frontier. From 251 and The Vagina Monologues to The F Word and Victor/Victoria, her directing credits span drama, musical theatre and socially engaged work in Singapore, Canada, Edinburgh and internationally.

This instinct eventually moved from stage to page to sound to screen. Her books explore women, leadership, identity, motherhood and lives that exist beyond the conventional narrative. Her film work continues the same inquiry.

Different mediums. Same obsession:

Who gets seen? Who gets heard? Which stories need to be told?

06 · Off duty

My perfect weekend

“My perfect weekend starts before the rest of the island wakes up — somewhere between the ocean, a yoga mat, a long walk and eighteen hungry cats.”

Inspired by My Perfect Weekend, as told to The Straits Times and updated.

3–4 AM Sunrise at Makapuʻu Up absurdly early. A drive along the ocean toward the eastern tip of Oʻahu to watch the first light come over Makapuʻu. Always humbling to be reminded how small we are.
Dawn Meditate. Coffee. Breakfast. Meditation first followed by vanilla macadamia coffee and something worth waking up for. In Singapore: prata, lontong or mee pok with lots of chilli.
In Hawaiʻi: a mochi waffle at Koa Pancake House. Balance is a spiritual practice.
Morning Move. Yoga Sculpt or Core Strength X at CorePower Yoga, or F45 when I want someone else to make the decisions. After completing my 200-hour yoga teacher training at CorePower Yoga, yoga has become more than exercise: part strength, part mobility, part meditation and a reminder that practice matters more than perfection.
Late morning Read. Think. Wander. Leadership, humanity, technology, culture — usually several books at once and none necessarily from beginning to end. Ray Dalio. Ben Horowitz. Mustafa Suleyman. Erin Meyer. And whatever has distracted me from the book I was supposed to finish. See the nightstand ↗
Noon Lunch with 18 cats Blissful but they will also want food. All of them.
Sunday The long road. Sunday is marathon-training day: miles on foot, headphones on, one step after another. There is something wonderfully democratic about distance. You cannot negotiate with the next mile. You simply keep going.
Afternoon Ride. Listen. Reset. Thirty minutes on the bike with Prof G, Planet Money, TED or the BBC in my ears. Usually Jo Stafford, Etta James or Billie Holiday but Madonna, David Guetta, Calvin Harris when the brain needs cardio.
Night Lights out. Early-ish. Four or five cats in the bed particularly Phoenix and Noona, who remain convinced that the best place to sleep is with their butt on my face. Perfect? 100%.

Book the force of nature. Keynotes, advisory, education.
